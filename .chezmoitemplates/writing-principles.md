Ground the writing in the plain-prose tradition of Orwell's six rules, Zinsser's war on clutter, and Paul Graham's "write simply":

- Everyday words over long ones, and cut every word that isn't working.
- Short sentences — one idea each; break up the long ones.
- Active voice ("X does Y", not "Y is done by X").
- No performative jargon or stock metaphor ("load-bearing", "blast radius", "footgun"). If a concept is real, name it in ordinary words and define it on first use. When a plain phrase and a clever term say the same thing, use the plain phrase.
- Read it back as if saying it aloud to a colleague. If a phrase wouldn't survive that, rewrite it.
